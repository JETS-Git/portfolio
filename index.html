Design rationale

How I choose a pathway

Most learning problems do not need the most impressive technology. I start from the performance gap and work upward only when a cheaper option cannot close it.

  1. Name the gap

    Is the problem knowledge, skill, confidence, or the system around the learner? Each points to a different solution.

  2. Pick the lowest rung that works

    A pocket card beats a course if the need is recall at the bedside. Simulation is reserved for judgement under pressure.

  3. Build with learners

    Prototype early, put it in front of students, and change it based on what they do, not what they say they will do.

  4. Map, moderate, evaluate

    Tie every item to the unit's evidence requirements and NMBA standards, then collect evidence that it changed something.

Background

Educator first, informed by clinical practice

My work is teaching and learning design. At Marr Mooditj Training I deliver the Diploma of Nursing, design its digital learning and assessment, and lead the move from paper-based assessment to Moodle. I build simulations and AI learning tools, and I am deepening my practice through a Graduate Certificate in Education (Digital Learning), HERDSA’s SoTL modules and an Advance HE Fellowship application.

Clinical practice keeps that work honest. I facilitate nursing students on placement at Fiona Stanley and Royal Perth hospitals and work casual CCU and ED shifts, so I design for the moment a student meets a real patient.

Before nursing I spent thirteen years as a commissioned Intelligence Officer in the Royal Australian Air Force. Analysing systems, weighing options under uncertainty and writing the reasoning down for someone else to scrutinise are habits from that career, and they are the habits this portfolio documents.

My research follows the same thread: making reasoning visible. I have applied for doctoral study at the University of Notre Dame Australia from 2027. The proposed research, Making Clinical Reasoning Visible, examines how clinical reasoning can be assessed in OSCEs. A later strand explores game mechanics that make a learner's diagnostic reasoning visible step by step.
